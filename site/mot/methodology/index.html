How we calculate the MOT figures

Updated 25 November 2026

The figures come from DVSA's anonymised MOT test results for Great Britain (2025), published under the Open Government Licence. Motorcycles are test classes 1 and 2. We download every year, keep the motorcycle tests and their defects, and count them as below.

Definitions

  • Annual MOT. A bike's first normal test in a calendar year. Retests, appeals and aborted tests are left out, so a bike that fails and is fixed counts once, as a fail.
  • Failed first time. The annual MOT's result was a fail, including "PRS": failed, fixed and passed while still at the test station.
  • Failed on a part. The annual MOT had at least one fail or dangerous defect on that part. Shown as a share of all annual MOTs of that model, so 4% means 4 in 100 MOTs failed on it. This is not the same as a part's share of all faults.
  • Advisory. An item the tester noted as worn or needing attention, but that did not fail the bike.
  • Age. Test year minus the year the bike was first used. Mileage is the odometer reading at the MOT; implausible readings are left out.
  • Class average. The same figure for all bikes with an engine of the same size band: up to 125 cc, 126-500 cc, 501-900 cc, over 900 cc, or electric. Highlighted when a model is at least 1.25 times or at most 0.8 times the average.

Which bikes count as one model

DVSA stores the model name as it was typed when the bike was registered, so one bike appears under many spellings and a name like "MT" can mean different bikes. We group records into a model family by make, the first word of the model name and the engine size:

  • Names are compared without hyphens and without an ABS, A, SP or SE ending after the model number (MT-07, MT07 and MT-07A are one bike).
  • A number that only repeats the engine size is ignored (Tiger 1050 at 1,050 cc is a Tiger).
  • Engine sizes of one name within 3% of each other count as one engine (1,280 and 1,300 cc).
  • A bare name ("MT" at 690 cc) joins a longer name that starts with it ("MT-07") when that name holds at least 80% of the longer names at that engine size. Otherwise it stays on its own.

Each page lists the names it includes. Models need at least 100 annual MOTs for a page.

Limits

  • The MOT checks safety items (brakes, lights, tyres, steering, suspension, structure). It does not test engines, gearboxes or electronics, so those failures are not here. For those, see the US complaints and owner reports.
  • Bikes only need an MOT from three years old, so new models have few results.
  • Owners who maintain their bikes before the test fail less, and that is not spread evenly across models.
