Other LiveWire recalls (1)

Recalls for models with no matching owner complaints. The model name is as the maker filed it.

S2 MULHOLLAND · High Voltage Fuse May Cause a Loss of Drive Power (electrical and instruments): 2024 · 24V601000 · 2024-08-12 · 788 bikes

Problem. LiveWire EV, LLC (LiveWire) is recalling certain 2024 S2 Del Mar and S2 Mulholland motorcycles. The high voltage fuse may degrade and unexpectedly open, resulting in a loss of drive power.

Risk. Unexpected loss of drive power increases the risk of a crash.

Fix. Dealers will install a high voltage fuse with an increased amperage rating, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed August 2024. Owners may contact LiveWire customer service at 1-855-387-4337. LiveWire's number for this recall is 1003.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Repeated rapid acceleration events can degrade the high voltage fuse, which may result in the fuse opening.

What the new part or software changes. A high voltage fuse with an increased amperage rating is included in the recall kit.

Fixed in production. On approximately 9/11, production with the new high voltage fuse with an increased amperage rating is expected to begin.

Part made by: LiveWire EV, LLC, United States · Maker's recall no. 1003

How the problem was found.

  • 06/11/2024 - LiveWire’s Service organization was notified of a report involving an alleged unexpected loss of propulsion on a LiveWire motorcycle.
  • 06/25/2024 - The Technical Subcommittee (TSC) launched a formal investigation of this issue.
  • 06/26/2024 - 07/15/2024 - The TSC conducted a warranty/field service review, which identified two additional reports of a loss of propulsion that appeared to be potentially related to this issue.
  • 07/16/2024 – The TSC concluded its investigation and escalated the matter to the Field Action Committee (FAC).
  • 07/26/2024 – The FAC reviewed the TSC’s findings and analysis and escalated the matter to LiveWire’s Executive Decision Authority (EDA).
  • 08/05/2024 – Based upon the information developed during the investigation, the EDA authorized a recall to address this condition. There have been no reports of crashes or injuries related to this issue.
