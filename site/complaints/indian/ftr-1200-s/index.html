Altered Fuel Tank May Leak Fuel (fuel system): 2022 · 24V860000 · 2024-11-14 · 4 bikes

Problem. Indian Motorcycle Company (Indian) is recalling certain 2022 FTR 1200 R Carbon, FTR 1200, FTR 1200 S and FTR 1200 Rally motorcycles. These motorcycles may have been assembled with an altered fuel tank containing holes that can leak fuel.

Risk. A fuel tank leak increases the risk of fire or injury.

Fix. Dealers will replace the fuel tank, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 17, 2024. Owners may contact Indian customer service at 1-877-204-3697. Indian's number for this recall is I-24-09.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The fuel tank may have been altered prior to vehicle sale resulting in holes being present in the tank.

What the new part or software changes. Remedied components will not have been altered prior to installation.

Maker's recall no. I-24-09

How the problem was found.

  • On September 26, 2024 IMC received a dealer report of a hole present in a fuel tank. On October 10, 2024 IMC reviewed internal records and determined the vehicle had been previously altered during vehicle certification testing. Thereafter, additional review was conducted of units that had undergone the same certification testing, resulting in the identification of 4 total vehicles that were …
  • November 7, 2024, IMC’s Executive Review Committee decided that the altered fuel tank concern resulted in a safety-related defect.

Brake Lights May Illuminate Unexpectedly/FMVSS 108 (lights): 2019-2020, 2022 · 24V069000 · 2024-02-02 · 11,648 bikes

Problem. Indian Motorcycle Company (Indian) is recalling certain 2020-2022 Challenger, Challenger Dark Horse, Challenger Limited, 2022 Challenger Dark Horse Icon, Challenger Elite, FTR 1200, FTR 1200 Rally, FTR 1200 S, FTR 1200 R Carbon, Pursuit Dark Horse, Challenger JD Limited Edition, Pursuit Limited Icon, Pursuit Dark Horse Premium, Pursuit Limited, Pursuit Limited Premium, 2019-2020 FTR 1200 S, FTR 1200, and 2020 FTR 1200 Rally motorcycles. The brake lights may illuminate unexpectedly and without brake application from the rider. As such, these motorcycles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."

Risk. Unintended illumination of the brake lights may create confusion for the drivers following the motorcycle, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Dealers will update the ABS module software, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed March 1, 2024. Owners may contact Indian customer service at 1-877-204-3697. Indian's number for this recall is I-24-03.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Due to an error in the ABS module software, when wheel speed differentials exceed the calibrated threshold, the ABS module software may cause the ABS module to provide a signal to the ECM, which then illuminates the brake lights even when there is no brake application by the motorcycle user.

Fixed in production. As of April 13, 2022, all product leaving an Indian Motorcycle manufacturing facility have the correct ABS module software and thus do not have this inadvertent brake light illumination issue.

Part made by: Robert Bosch LLC, United States · Maker's recall no. I-24-03

  • On Sep 28, 2021, during routine review, Polaris found a social media post about brake light issues. An investigation was commenced (Oct '21 to late-Mar '22) resulting in Polaris’ determination that the brake light issue was caused by a software error in a supplier component, the Bosch ABS 9.1M Plus module.   • On April 13, 2022, Indian Motorcycle (Polaris) reported the noncompliance to NHTSA (later amending its report on Sep 9, 2022).

Hot Coolant May Leak on Rider and Rear Tire (engine and cooling): 2019-2020 · 22V636000 · 2022-08-23 · 6,315 bikes

Problem. Indian Motorcycle Company (Indian) is recalling certain 2019-2020 and 2022 FTR 1200, 2019-2020 FTR 1200 S, and 2020 FTR 1200 Rally motorcycles. The coolant junction may leak.

Risk. Hot coolant leaking on a rider can increase the risk of injury. Coolant leaking on the rear tire can increase the risk of a crash.

Fix. Dealers will replace the coolant junction, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed September 28, 2022. Owners may contact Indian customer service at 1-877-204-3697. Indian's number for this recall is I-22-04.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Failure of the coolant junction causing a leak during normal vehicle operation.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy coolant junction part will have been manufactured subject to a material change to increase performance at operating temperatures and reduce manufacturing variation.

Fixed in production. As of August 19, 2022, Polaris stopped shipment of units with the suspect coolant junction part and shipment will only resume when the updated coolant junction part is available.

Part made by: RL Hudson, United States · Maker's recall no. I-22-04

How the problem was found.

  • In February 2022, Indian Motorcycle identified a leak at the coolant junction on an FTR motorcycle in development testing that resulted in hot coolant contacting the test rider. The test rider was not injured. Thereafter, Indian Motorcycle initiated an investigation, which included outreach to the component part supplier and testing performed internally and at the supplier. During the …
  • On August 16, 2022, Indian Motorcycle’s Executive Review Committee decided that the failure at the coolant junction resulting in coolant loss during operation resulted in a safety-related defect.
