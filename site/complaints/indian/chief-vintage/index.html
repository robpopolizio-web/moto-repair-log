Recalls (1)

Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

Fuel system: Fuel rail may chafe and leak: 2014-2017 · 16V877000 · 2016-12-06 · 23,761 bikes

Problem. Indian Motorcycle Company (Indian) is recalling certain model year 2014-2017 Chief Classic, Chief Dark Horse, Vintage, Chieftan, Chieftan Dark Horse, Springfield and Roadmaster motorcycles manufactured April 15, 2013, to July 15, 2016. In the affected motorcycles, the fuel rail may contact other components and result in a fuel leak.

Risk. A fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source increases the risk of a fire.

Fix. Indian will notify owners, and dealers will install a bracket that will limit movement of the fuel rail, free of charge. The recall began on December 12, 2016. Owners may contact Indian customer service at 1-877-204-3697. Indian's number for this recall is I-16-03.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The fuel rail tubes are flexible and allow the rail to inadvertently be re- positioned. This may lead to contact with other components during service or manufacture.

What the new part or software changes. Remedy bracket is an additional component that limits potential movement of the fuel rail, preventing contact with adjacent components. Remedy fuel rails utilize tighter manufacturing tolerances at joints.

Fixed in production. Additional clearance was created to adjacent components, eliminating the opportunity for fuel rail contact.

Part made by: Indian Motorcycle / Polaris Industries, United States · Maker's recall no. I-16-03

How the problem was found.

  • Polaris initiated a full scale root cause analysis on fuel rail failures observed in warranty reviews. Polaris began testing for root cause and monitoring any subsequent occurrences. Based upon an analysis of field claims and a thorough evaluation of the components in the system a decision was made to perform a safety recall for the fuel rail. Recalls in Canada and Japan (11)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Canada: Chief Vintage 2026 · 2026-06-19 · no. 2026305 · 46 bikes · Label

On certain motorcycles, the compliance label may list the wrong rim sizes. As a result, when the rims are replaced, the wrong rims could be installed. This could cause poor handling or a tire failure. Safety Risk: Poor handling or a tire failure could cause a loss of vehicle control and result in a crash. Corrective Action: Indian Motorcycle will mail a new compliance label to each owner. If you would like help to install it, you can contact an Indian Motorcycle dealer to have it installed free of charge.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Chief Vintage 2016 · 2026-06-12 · no. 2026298 · 2,322 bikes · Structure

On certain vehicles, the windscreen glass may not have the proper markings required by the Canadian regulations. Safety Risk: This small error does not affect safety. Corrective Actions: This is not a recall. The company has made changes to ensure that all new vehicles have a compliant windscreen.

Source: Transport Canada

Japan: Chief Vintage · 2021 · no. 001416505.pdf · 231 bikes

Gear position sensor internal resistance of gear position switch is improperly set, an inaccurate gear number signal is transmitted due to resistance value cannot be detected correctly, and the meter may display the wrong number of gears. Therefore, the gear was shifted to any position, in spite of the neutral is displayed, and in the worst case, the driver may mistakenly recognize the neutral and fall. All vehicles replace gear position switches with updated product. Also inspect the shift drum pin and spring and replace them with new ones if they are damaged.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Canada: Chief Vintage 2014-2017 · 2016-12-12 · no. 2016616 · 1,491 bikes · Fuel System

On certain motorcycles, the fuel rail could shift positions and could contact other components and chafe, causing a fuel leak. Leaking fuel, in the presence of an ignition source, could lead to a fire causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will install a bracket that limits movement of the fuel rail. Additionally, dealers will inspect fuel rails for damage and replace as necessary.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Chief Vintage 2014-2015 · 2016-06-27 · no. 2016327 · 1,183 bikes · Engine

On certain motorcycles, the engine may misfire and cause unburned fuel to be routed into the exhaust system. This could result in excessive exhaust heat that may pose a fire hazard which could cause injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will update the ECU with software that includes misfire detection.

Source: Transport Canada

Japan: Chief Vintage · 2016 · no. 001127388.pdf · 15 bikes

Some 2015 - 2016 Indian Full-Size motorcycles have been manufactured with a rear brake master cylinder that does not meet Indian Motorcycle quality standards. In some vehicles, the rear brake master cylinder piston cup may develop a tear. Motorcycles assembled with an affected master cylinder may experience a loss of fluid pressure to the rear brake caliper resulting in reduced rear brake performance. This could cause the vehicle operator to lose control, increasing the risk of a crash.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Canada: Chief Vintage 2015 · 2015-11-05 · no. 2015526 · 431 bikes · Brakes

On certain motorcycles, the primary cup seal in the rear master cylinder could tear. If this were to occur, brake fluid pressure to the rear caliper would be reduced, and the performance of the rear brake would be degraded. This would increase stopping distances and could affect braking control, which could increase the risk of a crash causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will replace the rear brake master cylinder piston assembly.

Source: Transport Canada

Japan: Chief Vintage · 2014 · no. 001054578.pdf · 68 bikes

The rear rim used on affected vehicles may have the less cross-sectional thickness that does not meet specification because of the improper manufacturing. Therefore, the rim may crack and cause a loss of air pressure while riding. This could cause the vehicle operator to lose control and stability.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Canada: Chief Vintage 2003 · 2003-11-27 · no. 2003298 · 78 bikes · Lights And Instruments

On certain motorcycles, the bracket used to support the teardrop headlight can crack and/or break causing the light to slip out of position or fall off. This could result in a loss of visibility to the driver, increasing the risk of a crash. Correction: Since Indian Motorcycles is no longer in business, no correction is available.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Chief Vintage 2002-2003 · 2003-11-27 · no. 2003300 · 48 bikes · Fuel System

On certain motorcycles, the gas tank vent tube can become clogged with debris and could cause an interruption of fuel flow to the carburetor and the engine to stop. A clogged vent tube may also cause pressure to build up in the gas tank and push a small amount of gas out of the fuel filler cap seal. Fuel leakage in the presence of an ignition source can result in a fire. Correction: Since Indian Motorcycles is no longer in business, no correction is available.

Source: Transport Canada
