Recalls (2)

Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

Brakes: 1986 · 86V091000 · 1986-07-11 · 3,743 bikes

Problem. Heavy load and/or highly compressed rear suspension could cause the rear brake hose to contact the passenger's right footpeg securing stud, possibly fraying the hose. Consequence of defect: the hydraulic brake hose could wear through, causing a leak and resulting in the failure of the rear brake system. Failure of the rear brake system may contribute to an accident and personal injury.

Risk.

Fix. Install additional guide on the brake hose holder to prevent excess brake hose travel. Also, install a modified passenger footpeg.

Check if your VIN is affected on nhtsa.gov

Fuel hose or line: 1986 · 86V090000 · 1986-07-11 · 3,743 bikes

Problem. The distance between the fuel cock and carburetor may be insufficient to allow proper clearance for the outlet fuel line causing it to chafe. Consequence of defect: continued chafing could cause the fuel line to fail, allowing gasoline to leak which may result in a fire if a source of ignition is present.

Risk.

Fix. Install modified fuel cock that allows additional fuel hose clearance.

Recalls in Canada and Japan (1)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Canada: Fj1200 1986 · 1986-07-16 · no. 1986076 · 747 bikes · Brakes

There are two defects in this recall. (1) the outer protective covering of the rear brake hose connecting the the rear brake master cylinder to the rear brake caliper could contact the bolt retaining the right hand passenger footpeg, causing abrasion of the outer covering. This could lead to brake fluid leakage and loss of rear brakes. (2) the fuel hose connecting the gas tank fuel cock to the carburetors could contact the front of the #2 carburetor causing possible abrasion and ultimate fuel leakage.

Source: Transport Canada
