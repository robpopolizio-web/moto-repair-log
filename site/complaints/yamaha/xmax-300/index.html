Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix.

Engine and cooling: 2018-2022 · 25V778000 · 2025-11-12 · 2,715 bikes

Problem. Yamaha Motor Company, Ltd. (Yamaha) is recalling certain 2018-2022 XMAX scooters. The camshaft retainer bolt may loosen and not properly secure the camshaft, resulting in an engine stall.

Risk. An engine stall can cause a loss of control, increasing the risk of a crash or injury.

Fix. Owners are advised not to ride their scooter until the remedy has been completed. Dealers will install a new camshaft plate kit, free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed November 20, 2025. Owners may contact Yamaha's customer service at 1-800-962-7926. Yamaha's number for this recall is 990184. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning November 24, 2025.

Check if your VIN is affected on nhtsa.gov

Mirrors and windscreen: 2018-2020 · 20V611000 · 2020-10-05 · 31,000 bikes

Problem. Yamaha Motor Corporation, USA (Yamaha) is recalling certain 2015-2020 YZF-R3, 2020 MT03 and 2018-2020 XMAX scooters. Condensation or other contaminates may adhere to the rear reflector lens reducing its effectiveness.

Risk. A less reflective rear reflector increases the risk of crash.

Fix. Yamaha will notify owners, and dealers will replace the rear reflector, free of charge. The recall began October 20, 2020. Owners may contact Yamaha customer service at 1-888-266-3085. Yamaha's number for this recall is 990139.

2021 Yamaha XMAX 300 · 2,900 miles · Engine and cooling · NY · filed 2026-07 · NHTSA 11751856The contact owns a 2021 Yamaha XMAX 300 Motorcycle. The contact stated that shortly after the motorcycle was repaired under NHTSA Campaign Number: 25V778000 (ENGINE AND ENGINE COOLING); while riding approximately 55 MPH, the motorcycle suddenly decelerated and stalled. The motorcycle was towed to a local mechanic nearby and the contact was informed that the cam shaft bolt was missing from the motorcycle. The motorcycle was later towed back to the local dealer, and the contact was informed that the cam shaft bolt had sheared off. The motorcycle was repaired by the dealer, but the contact was not provided the information about the repair. After retrieving and starting the motorcycle, engine oil immediately started pouring from the engine onto the ground. The cause of the failure was not yet determined. The manufacturer was notified of the failure. The failure mileage was 2,900.
