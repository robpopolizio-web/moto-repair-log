Headlight: Upper headlight beam is too bright/FMVSS 108: 2020, 2024 · 25V505000 · 2025-08-07 · 1,512 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor USA, LLC (Suzuki) is recalling certain 2020-2025 DL 1050 and 2020, 2024 GSX-1000S motorcycles. The upper headlight beam may be too bright and exceed the maximum light output, which may adversely affect the vision of the driver. As such, these vehicles fail to comply with the requirements of the Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 108, "Lamps, Reflective Devices, and Associated Equipment."

Risk. Headlights that are excessively bright can reduce visibility for oncoming drivers, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Suzuki dealers will replace the headlight assembly, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed on November 7, 2025. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at 1-800-934-0934. Suzuki's number for this recall is 3A06/3A07.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Protective tape applied from November 20, 2019 to the upper beam reflector base to prevent surface- to-surface wear caused the reflector to elevate approximately 0.5 degrees. This caused the light distribution to shift and as a result the luminous intensity was slightly increased at the test point, exceeding the maximum allowable cd limit.

What the new part or software changes. The method of physical identification of remedy components has not yet been determined and will be identified in an amended Part 573 report.

Fixed in production. The schedule to implement countermeasure parts has not yet been determined and will be identified in an amended Part 573 report.

Fix type: Repair · Part made by: Koito Manufacturing Co., Ltd., Japan · Maker's recall no. 3A06/3A07

How the problem was found.

  • 7/17/2025 Koito Manufacturing reported to Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) that certain headlamps were produced that did not meet the photometric requirements specified in FMVSS 108.
  • 7/25/2025 Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) conducted testing of headlamps affected by the defect and determined that although the maximum candela exceeded the standard in one test area, the overall impact to light emittance was slight.
  • 7/31/2025 Because the affected headlamps exceed the maximum photometric value for one test point, SMC decided to conduct a safety recall.

Brake caliper: Front brake calipers may leak brake fluid: 2016 · 15V788000 · 2015-11-23 · 600 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor of America, Inc. (Suzuki) is recalling certain model year 2016 GSX-S1000 motorcycles manufactured July 8, 2015, to July 29, 2015, GSX-S1000A motorcycles manufactured July 10, 2015, to August 24, 2015 and, and GSX-S1000FA motorcycles manufactured July 21, 2015, to July 27, 2015. The affected motorcycles may leak brake fluid from the front brake calipers.

Risk. If the front brake calipers leak brake fluid, braking performance would be reduced, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Suzuki has notified owners, and dealers will replace the front brake calipers, free of charge. The recall began on December 1, 2015. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at 1-714-572-1490. Suzuki's number for this recall is 2A48.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The supplier of the front brake caliper applied a surface treatment to change the color of the brake caliper body. Due to insufficient sealing, some treating liquid may have entered the brake piston cylinder, damaging the surface condition of the brake piston seal groove in the piston cylinder.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy component did not receive the surface treatment that caused the reported defect.

Part made by: FUJI CORPORATION, Ltd., Japan · Maker's recall no. 2A48

How the problem was found.

  • In September, 2015, Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) received an initial Field Technical Information Report (FTIR) from Suzuki Motor of America, Inc. (SMAI) about brake fluid leakage from the front caliper of a motorcycle in a dealer showroom. SMC investigated the collected parts and was able to reproduce the incident after long-term parking. SMC also investigated the production process and learned …
  • In October, 2015, based on investigation of the collected parts and reproduction of the reported brake fluid leakage, SMC theorized that the treating liquid that was used in the process of changing the color of the brake caliper body damaged the surface condition of the brake piston seal groove. SMC decided to continue the investigation. SMC received 23 FTIRs from SMAI in October, 2015.
  • On November 13, 2015, SMC decided to conduct a safety recall to address this issue. In November, 2015, SMC received 11 FTIRs from SMAI.
