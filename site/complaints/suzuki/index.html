BURGMAN 200 · Fuel hose or line: Fuel hose assembly may leak fuel: 2019-2020 · 21V558000 · 2021-07-22 · 270 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor USA, LLC (Suzuki) is recalling certain 2019-2020 Burgman 200 UH200 scooters. The fuel hose assembly may leak fuel.

Risk. A fuel leak increases the risk of fire.

Fix. Dealers will replace the fuel hose assembly, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed August 20, 2021. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at (714) 572-1490. Suzuki's number for this recall is 2A97.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Fuel hose of affected vehicles may have been manufactured using a resin-based extrusion mandrel that was larger than the inspection control value of the supplier. Fuel hose produced under this condition may have an internal diameter that is larger than the inspection control value of the supplier and may have an improperly loose connection with the metal fuel hose connection pipes.

What the new part or software changes. The countermeasure part uses fuel hose that has the correct internal diameter specification.

Fixed in production. From May 18, 2020, the fuel hose supplier has implemented new quality control measures that replace previous batch sample inspection of hoses to inspection of 100% of production.

Part made by: Technomeiji Rubber (Thailand) Co.,Ltd., Thailand · Maker's recall no. 2A97

BURGMAN 200 · Speedometer: Speedometer and odometer may fail: 2019-2020 · 21V299000 · 2021-04-28 · 270 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor USA, LLC (Suzuki) is recalling certain 2019-2020 UH200 motorcycles. The speedometer assembly resistors may corrode and cause a loss of power to the wheel speed sensor, preventing the speedometer and odometer from displaying information.

Risk. The operator will not know the vehicle speed without a functioning speedometer, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Dealers will replace the speedometer assembly, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed May 24, 2021. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at 714-572-1490 or www.suzukicycles.com.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The wheel speed sensor power supply circuit in the speedometer assembly may contain resistors that are prone to corrosion caused by a reaction to sulfur gas that is released as a natural byproduct from the rubber boot that covers the speedometer assembly wire harness coupler. If corrosion occurs, the affected resistors may develop poor continuity preventing supply of electrical power to the wheel speed sensor. If electrical power is lost, the wheel speed signal will not be sent to the speedometer assembly and the speedometer and odometer will not function.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy component can be distinguished by part number.

Fixed in production. Prior to October 2019, the speedometer assembly manufacturer obtained resistors from two different vendors for use in the speedometer circuit board. The resistors supplied by one of the two vendors were found to have insufficient resistance to corrosion and a resulting potential loss of circuit continuity. From October 2019 onwards, the speedometer supplier discontinued use of resistors sourced from the vendor that had supplied the subject parts, and from that point only used resistors sourced …

Part made by: Cte Tech Corp, Taiwan, Province of China · Maker's recall no. 2A96

GSX250R · Brake light and brake light switch: Rear brake lights may fail to illuminate: 2018-2019 · 19V418000 · 2019-06-05 · 2,640 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor of America, Inc. (Suzuki) is recalling certain 2018-2019 GSX250R motorcycles. Water intrusion may corrode the rear brake light switch causing the rear brake light to fail to illuminate or remain illuminated continuously when the brake is not applied.

Risk. A failure of the brake light to illuminate, or continued illumination when the brakes are not being applied, can increase the risk of a crash.

Fix. Suzuki will notify owners, and dealers will replace the rear brake stop light switch, free of charge. The recall began June 28, 2019. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at 1-800-934-0934. Suzuki's number for this recall is 2A90.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Internal component parts of the stop lamp switch are made of inappropriate material which can corrode if water gets inside the switch.

What the new part or software changes. The recalled component is the stop lamp switch assembly, part number 33740-45D00. The remedy component can be distinguished from the recalled component because, for the remedy component, the wiring cover is not crimped and it does not have a cap on the bottom tip.

Fixed in production. The recall condition was corrected in production for 2019 model year vehicles produced on December 28 and December 29, 2018. A rear brake stop lamp switch produced by a Japanese supplier was used.

Part made by: Yueqing Huayao Electric Co., Ltd., China · Maker's recall no. 2A90

GW250 · Wiring and brake light switch: Brake light switch corrosion causing short circuit: 2013, 2015, 2017 · 16V808000 · 2016-11-07 · 1,890 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor of America, Inc. (Suzuki) is recalling certain model year 2013, 2015, and 2017 GW250 motorcycles. Water may enter the main wiring harness at the rear brake light switch connectors, possibly corroding the brake light switch.

Risk. The corrosion may cause an electrical short and the loss of the head light, tail light and brake lights, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Suzuki will notify owners, and dealers will install an improved brake light switch assembly, and install a water protection cover, as necessary, free of charge. The recall began on November 28, 2016. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at 1-714-572-1490. Suzuki's number for this recall is 2A66.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The main wiring harness/rear brake light switch connectors have insufficient protection from water intrusion.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy brake light switch assembly contains a water-protection cover on the connector.

Maker's recall no. 2A66

How the problem was found.

  • In August, 2012, Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) received a Field Technical Information Report (FTIR) from Changzhou Haojue Suzuki concerning a headlamp that could not be activated.
  • In November, 2012, SMC investigated the collected parts and found that the inside of the rear brake light switch was corroded by water. The corrosion had caused a short circuit and melting of the circuit protection fuse, resulting in disablement of the headlamp, which is located on the same electrical circuit as the brake light. SMC judged that the problem was likely caused by improper customer …
  • In January, 2013, SMC received an FTIR from its Japanese distributor concerning a headlamp that could not be activated.
  • In October, 2014, SMC decided to improve the water protection of production motorcycles due to the increasing number of incidents. SMC added a water protection cover on the main wiring harness connector. As of October, 2014, SMC had received a total of twenty-eight FTIRs.
  • In April, 2016, SMC received an FTIR concerning a similar incident involving a vehicle that had been produced with a water protection cover on the main wiring harness connector.
  • In May, 2016, SMC investigated the collected parts and found that the inside of the rear brake light switch was corroded by water which had entered through an electrical wire. As of May, 2016, SMC had received a total of fifty-six FTIRs.
  • In August, 2016, SMC reproduced the incident and found that water splashed from under the motorcycle could enter into the main wiring harness/brake light switch connectors. SMC judged that the improvement of
  • October, 2014 was insufficient. On October 28, 2016, SMC decided to conduct a safety recall to address the issue. As of October 28, 2016, SMC had received a total of sixty-two FTIRs, none of which were from the U.S. market.

GSX-S1000A, GSX-S1000FA · Brake caliper: Front brake calipers may leak brake fluid: 2016 · 15V788000 · 2015-11-23 · 600 bikes

Problem. Suzuki Motor of America, Inc. (Suzuki) is recalling certain model year 2016 GSX-S1000 motorcycles manufactured July 8, 2015, to July 29, 2015, GSX-S1000A motorcycles manufactured July 10, 2015, to August 24, 2015 and, and GSX-S1000FA motorcycles manufactured July 21, 2015, to July 27, 2015. The affected motorcycles may leak brake fluid from the front brake calipers.

Risk. If the front brake calipers leak brake fluid, braking performance would be reduced, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Suzuki has notified owners, and dealers will replace the front brake calipers, free of charge. The recall began on December 1, 2015. Owners may contact Suzuki customer service at 1-714-572-1490. Suzuki's number for this recall is 2A48.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The supplier of the front brake caliper applied a surface treatment to change the color of the brake caliper body. Due to insufficient sealing, some treating liquid may have entered the brake piston cylinder, damaging the surface condition of the brake piston seal groove in the piston cylinder.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy component did not receive the surface treatment that caused the reported defect.

Part made by: FUJI CORPORATION, Ltd., Japan · Maker's recall no. 2A48

How the problem was found.

  • In September, 2015, Suzuki Motor Corporation (SMC) received an initial Field Technical Information Report (FTIR) from Suzuki Motor of America, Inc. (SMAI) about brake fluid leakage from the front caliper of a motorcycle in a dealer showroom. SMC investigated the collected parts and was able to reproduce the incident after long-term parking. SMC also investigated the production process and learned …
  • In October, 2015, based on investigation of the collected parts and reproduction of the reported brake fluid leakage, SMC theorized that the treating liquid that was used in the process of changing the color of the brake caliper body damaged the surface condition of the brake piston seal groove. SMC decided to continue the investigation. SMC received 23 FTIRs from SMAI in October, 2015.
  • On November 13, 2015, SMC decided to conduct a safety recall to address this issue. In November, 2015, SMC received 11 FTIRs from SMAI.

GSX1300BK · Charging system (stator, regulator): 2008 · 11V108000 · 2011-02-23 · 73,426 bikes

Problem. Suzuki is recalling certain model year 2008 through 2010 motorcycles manufactured from july 2007 through september 2009, equipped with regulator/rectifier assemblies, suzuki part numbers 32800-41f11, 32800-15h10, 32800-05h11, 32800-41g10, 32800-15h00, 32800-18h00, 32800-05g10, 32800-10g10, 32800-05h20, or 32800-06g01. Some regulator/rectifier assemblies were produced with insufficient adhesion between the power module (circuit board) and the rectifier case that contains a heat sink to dissipate heat. Due to insufficient adhesion, heat generated on the power module circuit board can cause the circuit board to deform, and lift of the case.

Risk. This condition causes excessive heat on the circuit board and uncontrolled electric current output, which can result in insufficient charging current being provided to the motorcycle battery. This can cause discharge of the battery and can lead to engine stalling and/or a no-start condition. Engine stalling while riding can increase the risk of a crash.

Fix. Suzuki will notify owners and suzuki distributors will replace the regulator/rectifier with an improved part free of charge. The safety recall began on march 2, 2011. Owners may contact suzuki at 1-714-572-1490.
