Fuel hose or line: Fuel hose may disconnect and leak fuel: 2025 · 26V194000 · 2026-03-26 · 429 bikes

Problem. Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2025 CB1000SP motorcycles. The fuel feed hose may disconnect from the fuel tank, causing a fuel leak.

Risk. A fuel leak near the rear tire can increase the risk of a crash or injury. In addition, a fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source increases the risk of a fire.

Fix. Dealers will secure the angle sensor coupler wiring harness and replace the fuel hose, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed May 20, 2026. Owners may contact Honda's customer service at 1-866-784-1870. Honda's number for this recall is KU5. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning April 4, 2026.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The wiring harness design allowed for positional variation, and assembly processes did not include sufficient work instructions to control that variation. As a result, the angle sensor coupler may become positioned between the fuel feed hose and the turn signal relay during assembly, where it can apply force to the quick connector locking mechanism and cause it to release under operational vibration and fuel pressure, leading to disconnection of the fuel feed hose.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy component is the same as the recalled component. The component’s installation processes have been improved.

Fixed in production. The recall condition was corrected in production on August 26, 2025.

Fix type: Repair · Part made by: Honda Motor Co., Ltd. – Kumamoto Factory, Japan · Maker's recall no. KU5

How the problem was found.

  • May 23, 2025 Honda received report of the issue in Europe and began to investigate.
  • June 16, 2025 – September 30, 2025 Honda received additional reports in Europe and continued to investigate and analyze the issue.
  • November 3, 2025 Honda investigated and analyzed potential safety concerns with the issue.
  • November 13, 2025 – January 13, 2026 Honda received additional reports of the issue in Europe and Japan and continued to investigate and analyze the issue.
  • March 19, 2026 Honda determined that a defect related to motor vehicle safety existed and decided to conduct a safety recall. As of March 19, 2026, Honda has had no warranty claims and no report of injuries or death related to this issue in the US, from January 25, 2025, through March 19, 2026.

Engine internals: Engine rod bearing failure: 2025 · 25V892000 · 2025-12-18 · 474 bikes

Problem. Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2025 CB1000SP motorcycles. The pistons and piston rings may have been improperly manufactured, which can lead to rod bearing failure, possibly resulting in rear wheel lock up or oil leaking onto the rear tire.

Risk. Rear wheel lockup increases the risk of a crash or injury. Engine oil that leaks onto hot exhaust components increases the risk of a fire.

Fix. Dealers will inspect and repair the engine assembly as necessary, free of charge. Interim letters notifying owners of the safety risk were mailed February 9, 2026. Additional letters will be sent once the remedy is available, anticipated December 30, 2026. Owners may contact Honda's customer service at 1-866-784-1870. Honda's number for this recall is KT6. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning January 7, 2026.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The piston design and manufacturing method were changed from forging to casting, which required modifications to the piston geometry. These changes altered the oil jet flow pattern such that oil discharged from the oil jet may flow back toward the piston oil drain hole along the back surface of the piston, increasing the amount of oil present near the top piston ring. In addition, excessive piston ring side clearance can allow oil to accumulate around the top ring. Under certain operating conditions involving high engine vacuum, such as during deceleration or engine braking, this oil may be drawn into the combustion chamber. Over time, this condition can accelerate wear of the top ring surface treatment, further increase the side clearance and allowing additional oil migration into the combustion chamber, resulting in increased engine oil consumption.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy components have been redesigned with improved piston and top ring specifications and oil drain holes.

Fixed in production. The condition will be corrected in production in late March 2026.

Fix type: Repair · Part made by: Honda Foundry Co., Ltd., Japan · Maker's recall no. KT6, KU4

How the problem was found.

  • July 1, 2025 Honda received the initial report of high oil consumption in the European market and began to investigate the issue.
  • October 10, 2025 Honda received a repurchased vehicle experiencing the issue and continued to investigate and analyze the issue.
  • October 20, 2025 Honda received additional reports of the issue and continued to investigate and analyze the issue.
  • November 14, 2025 – December 10, 2025 Honda continued to analyze the issue.
  • December 11, 2025 Honda determined that a defect related to motor vehicle safety existed and decided to conduct a safety recall. As of December 11, 2025, Honda has had 7 warranty claims and no reports of an injury or death related to this issue from January 25, 2025, through December 11, 2025.
  • March 5, 2026 Honda concluded its investigation and analysis of the defect cause and remedy program.

Clutch lever: Loose gearshift pedal/FMVSS 123: 2025 · 25V723000 · 2025-10-23 · 340 bikes

Problem. Honda (American Honda Motor Co.) is recalling certain 2025 CB1000SP Hornet SP motorcycles. The pivot bolt on the gearshift pedal may loosen and detach, resulting in the inability to shift gears. As such, these motorcycles fail to comply with the requirements of Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) number 123, "Motorcycle Controls and Displays."

Risk. The inability to shift gears while riding increases the risk of a crash or injury.

Fix. Dealers will replace the pivot bolt, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed December 1, 2025. Owners may contact Honda's customer service at 1-866-784-1870. Honda's number for this recall is KT2. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will be searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning October 28, 2025.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Radiant heat from the nearby exhaust system can raise the temperature of the gear change pedal pivot bolt seat. The painted surface of the seat can deform when exposed to elevated temperatures, reducing the clamping force of the pivot bolt. Over time, the reduced clamping force can allow the pivot bolt to loosen and detach, resulting in the gear change pedal sagging and loss of gear shifting capability.

What the new part or software changes. The remedy consists of removing the paint coating at the pivot bolt seat and changing from a stepped bolt to a socket bolt with bolt collar and washer. In addition, a screw locking agent will be applied to prevent loosening.

Fixed in production. The recall condition will be corrected in production on November 3, 2025.

Fix type: Repair · Part made by: Saga Tekkohsho Co., Ltd., Japan · Maker's recall no. KT2

How the problem was found.

  • May 6, 2025 Honda received the first report of the issue in Europe.
  • June – July 2025 Honda received additional reports of the issue in Europe and began to investigate.
  • July - August 2025 Honda received additional reports of the issue from the European market and initial reports from Japan. Honda continued its investigation and analysis, which included the evaluation of returned parts from the first reported occurrence in Japan.
  • September 6, 2025 Honda received the first report of the issue in the US.
  • September 10, 2025 Honda continued to investigate and analyze the issue across all affected markets.
  • October 16, 2025 Honda determined that a defect related to motor vehicle safety and Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard noncompliance existed and decided to conduct a safety and noncompliance recall. As of October 16, 2025, Honda has had one warranty claim and no reports of an injury or death in the US related to this issue from January 25, 2025, through October 16, 2025.

Recalls in Canada and Japan (2)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Japan: Cb1000 Hornet · 2026-03-26 · no. 5796 · 620 bikes

In the electric wiring system, the main harness and the power relay cord may be inappropriately secured sometimes, which causes the angle sensor coupler connected to the main harness to interfere with the locking section of the fuel hose. For this reason, if the vehicle continues to be used as is, the fuel hose may come off, resulting in fuel leakage, which may lead to a fire in the worst case.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Japan: Cb1000 Hornet · 2026-03-19 · no. 5789 · 1,961 bikes

In the engine, due to improper designs of the piston and the top ring, the gap between them is excessively wide. As a result, when the engine repeatedly decelerates using engine braking from high RPM, engine oil may sometimes be drawn into the combustion chamber and burned, leading to increased oil consumption. For this reason, abnormal noise may occur due to the oil’s insufficient lubrication. If the vehicle continues to be operated without corrective action, the engine may fail while the vehicle is running, which can cause the rear wheel to lock and potentially lead to a tip-over or fire.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)
