Recalls (2)

Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

Brake master cylinder: Front brake master cylinder piston may crack: 2016-2018 · 18V033000 · 2018-01-10 · 2,361 bikes

Problem. KTM North America, Inc. (KTM) is recalling certain 2015-2016 1290 Super Duke R ABS, 2016 1290 Super Duke R SE ABS and 1290 Super Duke GT ABS and 2017-2018 Super Duke R and1290 Super Duke GT motorcycles. The piston within the Brembo front brake master cylinder may fail.

Risk. If the front brake master cylinder piston fails, the loss of front braking ability can increase the risk of a crash.

Fix. KTM will notify owners, and dealers will replace the piston with an aluminum one, free of charge. The recall began on February 21, 2018. Owner's may contact KTM customer service at 1-888-985-6090.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The anisotropy of the piston material, in addition to an unfavorable geometric distribution of porosity generated during the injection process, could lead to the failure of the component (crack generation) subject to stress typical of mission profiles such as: use of the vehicle on race tracks, frequent ABS interventions, or vehicle ground fall.

What the new part or software changes. The new piston is aluminum.

Fixed in production. The new piston is made from aluminium and machined from bar, guarantees a higher strength material as well as the absence of any possibility of porosity. This solution, already validated and in production for the past fifteen years, has proven to be fully reliable. In production line Brembo is now supplying the complete aluminum front brake master cylinder’s piston since September 2017.

Part made by: Brembo S.p.A, Italy · Maker's recall no. TB1805

How the problem was found.

  • October 2016 – Brembo now realizes that the above described condition may have first occurred during vehicle track use. No injuries reported. Brembo commenced the failure investigation.
  • February 2017 - Since it was not possible to identify the failure mode during the investigation jointly conducted with the motorcycle manufacturer the investigation was concluded. Monitoring of the field has been agreed.
  • June 2017 – A second field occurrence resulted in an investigation in which Brembo decided to introduce a preventative containment action on production replacing the PPS piston with an aluminium one (lead-time implementation of 2 months).
  • October 2017 - Two additional field occurrences were reported (both on the motorbikes track). No injuries have been reported so far.
  • November 2017 – Investigation closed: the root cause was identified as above (please refer to the “Describe the cause”).
  • December 2017 - Brembo decided to proceed with a word-wide safety recall campaign.

Fuel hose or line: Preformed fuel hose may develop holes or cracks: 2016-2017 · 17V194000 · 2017-03-21 · 487 bikes

Problem. KTM North America, Inc. (KTM) is recalling certain 2016-2017 KTM 1290 Super Duke GT motorcycles. The preformed fuel hose can develop small holes or cracks, potentially causing a fuel leak.

Risk. A fuel leak in the presence of a ignition source can increase the risk of a fire.

Fix. KTM will notify owners, and dealers will replace the defective fuel hose, free of charge. The recall began on May 5, 2017. Customers may contact KTM customer service at 1-888-985-6090.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Variations in the production process from the supplier of the preformed fuel hose.

Fixed in production. The supplier has modified the vulcanization process and stores proofed parts in marked yellow containers.

Part made by: ContiTech Gummi- und, Austria

How the problem was found.

  • November 9, 2016, KTM Sportmotorcycle GmbH determined the preformed hoses on affected units did not fulfill specifications. ContiTech investigated and found a variation in the manufacturing process. Recalls in Canada and Japan (2)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Canada: 1290 Super Duke Gt 2016-2017 · 2018-01-09 · no. 2018012 · 561 bikes · Brakes

On certain motorcycles, a crack within the polyphenilene sulphide (PPS) piston of the front brake master cylinder could occur. This could result in the master cylinder to not be able to generate sufficient hydraulic pressure, which could lead to a loss of braking capability of the front brake, increasing stopping distances and increasing the risk of a crash causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will replace the PPS piston with one made from aluminum.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: 1290 Super Duke Gt 2017 · 2017-03-24 · no. 2017174 · 133 bikes · Fuel System

On certain motorcycles, the preformed fuel hose may develop small holes or crack in the radius portion of the hose causing a fuel leak. A fuel leak in the presence of an ignition source could result in a fire causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will replace the preformed fuel hose.

Source: Transport Canada

2017 KTM Super Duke GT · 5,000 miles · Brakes & ABS · TN · filed 2018-03 · NHTSA 11082080I received nhtsa recall # 18v033 in regards to this vehicle. Ktm has failed to resolve this problem in a reasonable timeframe. The recall notice was dated on 2/23/2018. Additionally, brembo announced the problem on 1/8/2018. The dealership has ordered the parts as soon as they could, but they have yet to come in. There is not an estimated time frame for when the parts will arrive to allow for the repair. Given the repair has not been made 5 weeks after the notice and 12 weeks after the initial identification of the problem, ktm has not resolved the problem in a timely manner.
2016 KTM Super Duke GT · 1,464 miles · Engine · WA · filed 2017-08 · NHTSA 11019112I was riding my motorcycle on a scenic by-way with no shoulder travelling 30mph in a 40 mph zone. The motor turned itself off dash lights flashed and the bike would not restart. There was traffic behind me and I coasted to a stop where after several attempts to restart it finally did. But then it went into "limp mode" reduced power which the dealer had to clear.
2016 KTM Super Duke GT · 1,400 miles · Engine · TX · filed 2017-03 · NHTSA 10967127It has happened 4 times so far. It seems to happen about every thirty minutes of riding or so. Each time I had been waiting at a red light and when I started off and had made 2 or 3 shifts and was accelerating lightly the bike lost power suddenly and the motorcycle traction control warning started flashing on my information panel. The bike would only run at idle speed for anywhere from a few seconds to as much as 15 or 20 seconds and then go into a "limp in" mode which lets the throttle work a little. The power of the engine is then cut back considerably. The problem is that while it's going into limp mode, you have to just coast or pull over and wait until the bike has some power to get going again. Luckily it hasn't happened while I was in heavy traffic or had nowhere to pull over safely yet. If it did, I could be in a lot of trouble from any vehicle behind me who wasn't ready for me to be suddenly slowing down without warning. The bike has less than 1500 miles on it. I took it to the dealer and they said the exhaust control valve is sticking causing the limp in mode to be set. They don't have a solution to repair it yet.
