Recalls (2)

Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

Electronics software and fuel injector: Improper ECU setting may cause engine stall: 2018 · 17V774000 · 2017-12-01 · 9,914 bikes

Problem. Kawasaki Motors Corp., U.S.A. (Kawasaki) is recalling certain 2017-2018 Z125 PRO & PRO KRT Edition motorcycles, models BR125JHF and BR125JJF. The Electronic Control Unit (ECU) may have an improper setting for the fuel injection, allowing the engine to stall.

Risk. An engine stall can increase the risk of a crash.

Fix. Kawasaki will notify owners, and dealers will replace the ECU, free of charge. The recall began on December 21, 2017. Owners may contact Kawasaki customer service at 1-866-802-9381.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Improper setting of the ECU causes the combustion state to become unstable at small throttle applications when stopping or starting the vehicle.

What the new part or software changes. A white paint spot will be applied to the frame cross pipe located under the seat.

Fixed in production. KHI has started to use the new ECU in production.

How the problem was found.

  • July 2016 - Kawasaki Heavy Industries, Ltd (KHI) received a field claim from the Japan market (KMJ) regarding engine stall while vehicle is stopped.
  • November 2016 - KHI started an investigation of parts returned from the field, but could not detect the problem.
  • January 2017 - KHI started an investigation of the vehicle returned from field, but could not reproduce the failure.
  • August - October 2017 - KHI investigated other field claimed vehicles (total of 3 vehicles) for duplication testing. KHI found out that the engine may stall due to poor combustion state while operating a small throttle applications. Also, KHI confirmed the validity of the proposed countermeasure by changing the ECU program (ignition timing).
  • November 22, 2017 - KHI decided to initiate recall campaign.

Rear shock: Shock absorbers may leak oil and lose function: 2017 · 16V535000 · 2016-07-13 · 1,282 bikes

Problem. Kawasaki Motors Corp., U.S.A. (Kawasaki) is recalling certain model year 2017 Kawasaki Z125 Pro motorcycles manufactured February 4, 2016 through June 7, 2016. In the affected motorcycles, the shock absorbers may leak oil and lose their function.

Risk. Loss of function of the shock absorbers may affect the motorcycle's handling, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Kawasaki will notify owners, and dealers will replace the shock absorbers, free of charge. The recall began July 26, 2016. Owners may contact Kawasaki customer service at 1-866-802-9381. Kawasaki's number for this recall is MC16-04.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The use of improper grease during the shock absorber manufacturing process.

What the new part or software changes. A pink mark is painted on the new shock absorber.

Fixed in production. KHI started to use new shock absorbers for production starting June 9, 2016.

Maker's recall no. MC16-04

How the problem was found.

  • April 2016 - Kawasaki Heavy Industries, Ltd (KHI) received information from the Japanese distributor about a potential oil leakage issue with the shock absorber on Kawasaki owned vehicles.
  • April 2016 - KHI inspected Kawasaki owned vehicles in warehouses in Thailand and discovered that some oil adhered to the piston rod of the shock absorber.
  • April 2016 - KHI directed the vendor of the shock absorber to investigate the assembly process.
  • May 2016 - KHI received the investigation report from the vendor indicating that oil must have accumulated outside of the oil seal (about 0.5cc) and adhered to the piston rod. The vendor added an oil wiping step in the assembly process.
  • June 2016 - KHI requested the vendor to further investigate because a vehicle manufactured with the wiping process had oil adhesion on the piston rod.
  • June 2016 - KHI received the second investigation report from the vendor indicating the performance of the oil seal could be insufficient due to improper grease used in the shock absorber manufacturing process.
  • June 2016 - KHI received a report from vendor indicating that the new grease eliminates such oil leakage. The vendor concluded the cause was the grease used in the orginial assembly.
  • July 1, 2016 - KHI informs KMC of recall action.

Recalls in Canada and Japan (3)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Japan: Z125 Pro · 2023-03-17 · no. 5294 · 2,817 bikes

On certain eligible units, during manufacturing stage an improper heat treatment process may have been applied to the lens of the turn signal lamp due to a parts control issue. As a result, the turn signal lamp lens may develop cracking and not meet the Japanese Act safety regulations for road vehicles.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Canada: Z125 Pro 2017 · 2017-11-24 · no. 2017576 · 334 bikes · Engine

On certain motorcycles, the engine control programming could be incorrect. This could result in engine stalling at idle or low engine speeds, which could increase the risk of a crash causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will replace the fuel injection control module.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Z125 Pro 2017 · 2016-11-09 · no. 2016556 · 215 bikes · Suspension

On certain motorcycles, the rear shock absorber may have an oil leak due to a manufacturing defect. A defective rear shock absorber could affect vehicle handling and stability increasing the risk of a crash causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will replace the rear shock absorber.

Source: Transport Canada
