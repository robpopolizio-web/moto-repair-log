Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

Crankcase breather: Pressurized crankcase may eject oil: 2026 · 26V270000 · 2026-04-28 · 88,039 bikes

Problem. Harley-Davidson Motor Company (Harley-Davidson) is recalling certain 2024-2026 FLTRX, FLHX, 2025-2026 FXBR, FLFB, 2025 FLHXU, 2026 FLTRXL, FLTRT, FLHXL, and FLHLT motorcycles built with an Airbox Baseplate (part number 29000373). The airbox backplate breather port may be blocked, allowing pressure to build up inside the crankcase.

Risk. When the dipstick is removed while the crankcase is pressurized, oil may eject from the fill spout, increasing the risk of injury.

Fix. Dealers will inspect the breather port, and if the port is blocked, repair the blockage, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed May 14, 2026. Owners may contact Harley-Davidson customer service at 1-800-258-2464. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov April 16, 2025. Harley-Davidson's number for this recall is 0193.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Due to a supplier manufacturing issue, the breather port may contain plastic material blocking the passageway.

What the new part or software changes. No remedy components will be required. The majority of the affected population is expected to only require an inspection. Motorcycles which demonstrate the recall condition will be repaired without requiring additional parts.

Fixed in production. Vehicles built after 2/11/2026 have been inspected under a new inspection process to ensure they do not present the recall condition.

Fix type: Inspect, Repair · Part made by: Overdrive Acquisition LLC dba Walbro, United States · Maker's recall no. 0193

How the problem was found.

  • 4/3/2025: Corporate action request (CAR) 20845 initiated for a production quality issue pertaining to blocked baseplates. A quarantine material request (QMR) was implemented.
  • 9/11/2025: A new QMR was implemented, which involved a visual inspection of the breather port with a flashlight.
  • 2/12/2026: It was discovered that the prior visual inspections may not have identified 100% of defective parts, because the condition could present as a very thin layer of plastic which allowed light (but not vapor) to pass through. A revised process was implemented to physically inspect the breather port using a tool, with repair as needed.
  • 3/18/2026: H-D received a Service Request citing blockage of a breather port and injury to a dealer technician.
  • 3/24/2026 – Harley-Davidson’s Technical Subcommittee (TSC) reviewed the matter.
  • 3/26/2026: – 4/9/2026: H-D conducted an evaluation of the issue and scope. The TSC reviewed the records of a quality screening by physical inspection of a batch of production parts which identified up to 10% of the incoming parts exhibiting a blocked breather port.
