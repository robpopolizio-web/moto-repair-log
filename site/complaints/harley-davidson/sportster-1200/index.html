1988 Harley-Davidson Sportster 1200 · Clutch, gearbox and final drive · CA · filed 1998-11 · NHTSA 700667The transmission door on the 4-speed harley davidson sportster evolution model (produced from 1984-1991) has a bearing that supports the transmission mainshaft. The bearing gets worn to the point where the magnets on the inside of the clutch basket at the end of the mainshaft come in contact with the stator mounted over the transmission door. The result is that the clutch basket magnets break up and the gears in the transmission start to shatter. This can cause the transmission to lock up, causing a loss of power and control of the motorcycle. Another result is that, if the magnets inside the clutch basket don't cause the transmission to shatter, the electrical system, because the magnets are broken, ceases to put out enough dc power to both run the motorcycle and recharge the battery. This will cause the motorcycle to not have enough charge in the battery to restart the motorcycle after you park it, leaving you stranded. This has happened to my motorcycle three (3) times in less than 30,000 miles. Harley davidson redesigned the charging system in 1992 and moved the charging system to the front crankshaft, thus eliminating the problem. However, there are approximately 80,000 to 100,000 of this model of 4-speed evolution sportster still on the road. There was a lawsuit filed in texas in approximately 1985 alleging exactly the same complaints. Please help. Thank you.
