Recalls in Canada and Japan (9)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Canada: Cvo Limited 2020-2022 · 2022-10-27 · no. 2022614 · 11,836 bikes · Brakes

On certain motorcycles, the brake lamp(s) could stay on after you release the brakes. Safety Risk: A brake lamp that stays on when the motorcycle isn't braking could cause confusion for other drivers and increase the risk of a crash. Corrective Actions: Harley-Davidson will notify owners by mail and instruct you to take your motorcycle to a dealership to update the motorcycle software. For Trike models, the dealer will also replace the brake switch.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Cvo Limited 2014-2022 · 2022-08-15 · no. 2022463 · 264 bikes · Lights And Instruments

This recall is for certain motorcycles equipped with an accessory detachable Tour-Pak luggage carrier conversion kit. The turn signal telltale may flash quickly when the Tour-Pak luggage isn't mounted, like when a bulb fails. As a result, you may not get warning when a turn signal bulb actually fails. Canadian regulations require a change in the turn signal flash rate when a bulb fails. Note: This recall affects Harley-Davidson accessory Tour-Pak luggage carrier conversion kit part numbers 53000916 and 53000917. These parts may have been installed on new motorcycles or sold as accessory parts through Harley-Davidson dealerships. Safety Risk: A turn signal telltale that doesn't work properly …

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Cvo Limited 2016-2019 · 2019-08-07 · no. 2019383 · 4,750 bikes · Accessories

On certain motorcycles with a "H-D Detachables Tour-Pak" luggage conversion kit, the rear reflectors may be missing. Canadian regulations require motorcycles to have rear reflectors. Note: This recall affects the following part numbers: 53000291, 53000291A and 53000567. Safety Risk: A motorcycle that does not have rear reflectors may be less visible to others and could increase the risk of a crash. Corrective Actions: Harley-Davidson will notify owners by mail and instruct them to take their vehicle to a dealer to install reflectors as necessary.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Cvo Limited 2017-2018 · 2018-10-19 · no. 2018572 · 12,750 bikes · Powertrain

On certain motorcycles, hydraulic fluid could leak past the clutch secondary actuator piston/seal. Over time, the loss of fluid could create an inability disengage the clutch. This may lead to a loss of control when started in gear, first shifted into gear, or while coming to stop, increasing the risk of a crash causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will install a new clutch secondary actuator piston assembly.

Source: Transport Canada

Canada: Cvo Limited (Flhtkse) 2014 · 2014-09-10 · no. 2014391 · 5,741 bikes · Powertrain

On certain motorcycles, the hydraulic clutch system may develop a tear in the primary cup (seal) on the clutch master cylinder piston, if this occurs, the system may lose the ability to maintain sufficient lift to keep the clutch disengaged. If this condition remains undetected, it could allow the motorcycle to "creep" forward from a stop, which could lead to a loss of control of the vehicle possibly causing a crash causing injury and/or damage to property. Correction: Dealers will rebuild the clutch master cylinder with a recall repair kit.

Source: Transport Canada

What owners wrote

The 6 most recent complaints, as filed (lightly reformatted).

2018 Harley-Davidson CVO Limited · 8,900 miles · Engine · TX · filed 2020-02 · NHTSA 11310386Engine suddenly lost power and became very hot after sustained highway speeds (75mph), when shifted down to pass another vehicle. In addition to the loss of power vehicle fuel consumption increased dramatically. Dealer found engine experienced engine oil sumping, whereby the engine oil pump was not returning sufficient oil to the oil tank. Excess oil was collecting in the bottom of the crank case. This oil would build up to the point that the crank would come into contact with the excess and cause foaming. The resulting lack of lubrication has caused significant internal engine damage. Harley davidson has known about this problem since the introduction of their 2017 models. They have come out with numerous design changes to the oil pump, however, the sumping has continued through the 2017, 2018, and 2019 model years. In 2020 they have now come out with yet another pump design which they contend will fix the problem. In the mean time harley has not conducted a recall on the prior models, leaving the entire 3 years of production of touring models subject to loss of power during critical periods, such as accelerating to pass another vehicle. Harley recommends that the oil pump be replaced at owner cost if engine upgrades are added, but they will not replace the pump at the company's expense until internal engine damage occurs. It appears that they are attempting to wait out for the expiration of the manufacturers original 2 year warranty.
