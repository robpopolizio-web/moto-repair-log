Recalls in Canada and Japan (3)

Recalls filed with Transport Canada and Japan's transport ministry for bikes listed under this model name. Many are the same fault as a US recall above; some were only issued there.

Japan: Cvo Road Glide Custom · 2023 · no. 001611118.pdf · 4,072 bikes

Regulatory Non-Compliance: We have learned that certain model year 2019 – 2022 Trike/CVO Trike motorcycles (see attached Addendum A) and certain model year 2020 – 2022 Touring/CVO Touring motorcycles (see attached Addendum A) may fail to conform to Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard/Canadian Motor Vehicle Safety Standard No. 108, Lamps, reflective devices, and associated equipment, in addition to other regulations including ECE R78. The subject motorcycles may exhibit a condition that can result in the brake lamp(s) remaining illuminated regardless of whether the brakes are being applied.

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Japan: Cvo Road Glide Custom · 2014 · no. 001059700.pdf · 2,936 bikes

On some of these motorcycles, the hydraulic clutch system may develop a tear in the primary cup (seal) on the master cylinder piston. If this occurs, the system may lose the ability to maintain sufficient lift to keep the clutch disengaged. If this condition remains undetected, it could allow the motorcycle to “creep” forward from a stop, which could lead to a loss of control of the vehicle possibly causing a crash. Harley-Davidson has initiated this recall to correct this condition in the affected motorcycles. Please keep in mind that this applies to your motorcycle even if you have replaced the original equipment clutch master cylinder with a Harley-Davidson Parts and Accessories master …

Source: Japan's transport ministry (MLIT)

Canada: Cvo Road Glide Custom (Fltrxse) 2012 · 2011-10-24 · no. 2011379 · 17,368 bikes · Electrical

On certain motorcycles, the proximity of the rear brake lamp switch to the exhaust system may induce heat into the brake switch that is beyond its temperature design limit. This could affect brake lamp function and/or cause a brake fluid leak through the switch. Failure of the brake lamp to illuminate when the brakes are applied may result in the following road users being unaware of the rider's intentions, increasing the risk of a crash. Brake fluid leakage could result in the loss of rear brake function which, in conjunction with traffic and road conditions, and the rider's reactions, could increase the risk of a crash. These issues could result in property damage and/or personal injury. …

Source: Transport Canada
