Recalls (1)

Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

Engine Failure (engine and cooling): 2024-2025 · 25V531000 · 2025-08-19 · 1,929 bikes

Problem. Triumph Motorcycles America, Ltd. (Triumph) is recalling certain 2024-2025 Daytona 660 motorcycles. The engine may fail due to internal damage from a disruption of oil flow.

Risk. Engine failure increases the risk of a crash.

Fix. Dealers will increase the engine oil capacity and install a redesigned dipstick, free of charge. Owner notification letters were mailed September 10, 2025. Owners may contact Triumph customer service at 1-678-854-2010. Triumph's number for this recall is SRAN 640.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. The root cause is insufficient lubrication within the crankshaft / bearing. This is triggered by air entering the oil system due to sloshing during aggressive deceleration, which causes pressure drops and disrupts oil flow.

What the new part or software changes. In controlled testing, the defect was consistently reproduced under specific conditions. However, when oil levels were increased as per the remedy, the issue was fully mitigated—no pressure drops were observed, and engine performance remained stable.

Fixed in production. Complete VIN range affected until end of production.

Fix type: Repair · Part made by: Triumph Motorcycles (Thailand) Ltd., Thailand · Maker's recall no. SRAN 640

2025 Triumph Daytona 660 · Engine and cooling · OR · filed 2026-09 · NHTSA 11767058The engine experienced catastrophic failure while riding the motorcycle at highway speeds - loss of power, a clicking noise, and then the engine died completely. Rider had to pull to the side of the freeway and coast to a safe location, engine would not restart. Motorcycle was then towed to the dealership who has evaluated the motorcycle and damage and opened a goodwill claim with Triumph corporate as the vehicle warranty expired at 20,000 miles (just prior to this incident occurring). The owner received the first and only recall notice for the low oil/short dipstick issue for this motorcycle on June 17th, 2026 via email - this was received five (5) days after the motorcycle was towed to the dealership on June 12th, 2026, and approximately 2.5 weeks following the engine's catastrophic failure. Warning lights showed up when the engine failed, but not prior. The manufacturer is refusing to replace/repair the engine due to the warranty being expired, even though the issue is due to the recall. I have only serviced this motorcycle with the dealership from which it was purchased (an authorized servicer of my motorcycle brand) and have maintained all records relating to services thus far (pre-sale inspection, 600 mile break-in service record, and 10k service record). The motorcycle was already on the schedule to be serviced for the 20k service interval. (10k service intervals for the Daytona 660, per the manufacturers recommendations). I also have written correspondence with the dealership regarding this issue and their communication with Triumph corporate.
