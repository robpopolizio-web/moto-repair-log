Unintended Fuel Injector Activation (fuel system): 2014 · 14V694000 · 2014-11-03 · 417 bikes

Problem. Triumph Motorcycles America, LTD (Triumph) is recalling certain model year 2014-2015 Triumph Thunderbird, Thunderbird ABS, Tiger 800,Tiger 800 ABS, Thruxton, and Bonneville motorcycles manufactured February 6, 2014, to August 7, 2014. The engine control unit may improperly activate the fuel injectors.

Risk. Improper signalling to, and activation of, the fuel injectors could cause the engine to unexpectedly lose power, increasing the risk of a vehicle crash.

Fix. Triumph will notify owners, and dealers will modify the internal ECU hardware, free of charge. The recall began December 4, 2014. Owners may contact Triumph customer service at 1-678-854-2010. Triumph's number for this recall is SB519.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Cause. Component failure within the ECU.

What the new part or software changes. Revised ECU for Tiger 800 model carries new part numbers T1299375. Other models do not have revised ECU part numbers.

Fixed in production. The production remedy was introduced on 7th August 2014 at Vin 676320. The production remedy is the same as that introduced in service.

Part made by: Keihin Europe LTD, United Kingdom · Maker's recall no. SB519

How the problem was found.

  • August 2014. Warranty data identifies issues in service. Dealer feedback requested. Subject parts requested for examination.
  • August – September 2014. Review of in service data and returned parts at Triumph an supplier. Rectification options identified, created and released for testing.
  • September 2014. Review of all data and preliminary testing conclusion. Best option in service rectification options identified. Production of counter measure parts commences.
  • October 10th 2014. Final review meeting between Triumph and supplier to review testing results. Agreement to conduct in service campaign.

Wheel Bearings may Fail Prematurely (wheels): 2012 · 13V032000 · 2013-01-29 · 250 bikes

Problem. Triumph Motorcycles (Triumph) is recalling certain model year 2011-2012 Daytona 675 and Street Triple motorcycles and 2012 Thunderbird and Thunderbird Storm motorcycles. The wheels were assembled with bearings of an unknown quality.

Risk. Wheel bearings of poor quality could fail unexpectedly, increasing the risk of a motorcycle crash.

Fix. Triumph will notify owners, and dealers will replace the affected bearings free of charge. Owners may contact Triumph at 1-678-854-2010 for more information.

2013 Triumph Thunderbird Storm · 12,000 miles · Clutch, gearbox and final drive · TX · filed 2015-11 · NHTSA 10794824Motorcycle has defectively designed carbon fiber belt drive system. Belt can easily be incorrectly adjusted which can cause the belt edge to be worn away, causing belt to snap at high speed. Basic problems include an inadequate adjusting mechanism and a custom blended coating on the drive pulley that causes the belt to make chirping noises and eventually wear the belt at an unreasonable speed if improperly adjusted. Factory specs state that the drive belt should run against the outer rim of the rear drive pulley rather than in the center. No other belt drive system is adjusted this way and that would be regarded as an incorrectly adjusted belt by any other belt drive manufacturer. Triumph has not made a belt drive system since the 1920's and has no experience in this area. Gates rubber company designed the belt drive system and the coating. The requirements were that the belt drive system last the live of the gates gt carbon fiber belt (150,000 miles). As an aside, most belt drive systems used by harley davidson (the only company to use belts on motorcycles successfully thus far) have rear drive pulleys that last about 50,000 miles. Harley davidson recommends that the belt and pulleys be replaced together at 50,000 mile intervals. The correct formula was turned down by triumph because the coating process produced a byproduct called hexavalent chromium which is heavily regulated by the epa. Triumph demanded another coating. Gates delivered it but advised against using it. Triumph covered early owner complaints under warranty due to pressure from dealers but when the true cause was discovered triumph ceased to admit it knew anything about the problem because it affects every thunderbird and thunderbird storm from 2009 to the present. The belt on this bike is supposed to be 32mm wide. After dealer adjustment, the belt (within 600 miles) is only 26mm wide.
