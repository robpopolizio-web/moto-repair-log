Recalls (5)

Newest first. Open one to read the problem, the risk and the fix, plus the cause and timeline from the maker's own defect report and links to the official documents.

BMW MOTORCYCLE/TRANSMISSION GEARBOX (clutch, gearbox and final drive): 2003 · 02V319000 · 2002-11-27 · 515 bikes

Problem. On certain motorcycles, it is possible that the 3rd and 4th transmission gear (shift) wheels may not be securely seated on the intermediate shaft. Consequently, when attempting to shift from 5th to 6th gear, engagement of the two gears may occur simultaneously.

Risk. If this happens, the gear wheels could crack resulting in a loss of transmission capability, or, a lock of transmission could occur. The rear wheel could lock which, depending upon traffic and road conditions, and the rider's reaction, could affect the stability of the motorcycle.

Fix. Dealers will inspect the motorcycles and replace the transmission gear box if necessary. Owner notification is expected to begin during december 2002. Owners who take their vehicles to an authorized dealer on an agreed upon service date and do not receive the free remedy within a reasonable time should contact bmw at 1-800-831-1117, option #4.

BMW/FUEL LINE CHAFING (fuel system): 1999 · 01V272000 · 2001-08-24 · 1,090 bikes

Problem. Vehicle description: motorcycles. The flexible fuel hose leading from the fuel distributor can chafe against the rigid lines of the fuel feed and the return lines. Continued chafing of the flexible fuel hose can lead to fuel leakage.

Risk. Fuel leakage, in the presence on an ignition source, could result in a fire.

Fix. Dealers will replace the right side flexible hose with one with a braided cover to prevent chafing. Owner notification began october 16, 2001. Owners who take their vehicles to an authorized dealer on an agreed upon service date and do not receive the free remedy within a reasonable time should contact bmw at 1-800-831-1117, option #5.

BMW/HANDLEBARS (steering): 1999-2001 · 00V318000 · 2000-10-11 · 718 bikes

Problem. Vehicle description: motorcycles equipped with high-mounted handlebars. The high-mounted handlebars are position on top of the upper fork clamp and are clamped to the top of each upper fork tube. It is possible for a handlebar to rotate forward.

Risk. If this were to occur while riding, the rider's balance could be upset and a crash and personal injury could result.

Fix. Dealers will degrease the clamping connection parts, rework the positioning tab (if necessary) to fit properly in the corresponding gap in the upper fork clamp, and reset the torque of the clamping connections.

BMW/REAR BRAKE LEVER (brakes and abs): 1999 · 00V144000 · 2000-05-18 · 474 bikes

Problem. Vehicle description: motorcycles. Due to use of an incorrect thread-locking material in production, it is possible for the rear brake linkage ball end, which is screwed into the rear brake lever, to loosen and eventually detach from the lever.

Risk. If this happens, the rear brake could no longer be applied, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. Dealers will remove the brake linkage ball end from the brake lever and reinstall it with a different thread-locking compound, to prevent the ball end from loosening.

BMW/FUEL LINE (fuel system): 1999 · 98V294000 · 1998-11-10 · 700 bikes

Problem. Vehicle description: motorcycles. If the engine backfires during the starting process, it is possible for either throttle body to loosen in its mount, or to disconnect from its mount. If this happens, the fuel line could be damaged or the line and injector could separate from each other, causing fuel leakage.

Risk. Fuel leakage in the presence of an ignition source can result in a fire.

Fix. Dealers will replace the fuel distribution unit. In addition, the connection between the throttle body and the air inlet tube will be secured with two screws to prevent damage to the hose or injector should a backfire occur.

2004 BMW R 1100 S · 33,861 miles · Clutch, gearbox and final drive, Fuel system · VT · filed 2014-09 · NHTSA 10631563I was traveling at 47mph and as I shifted from 4th to 5th gear, the bike suddenly lurched with a bang as it decelerated and I lost the ability to accelerate. The engine revved as if the bike was in neutral with the throttle engaged even though I should have been in 5th gear. I twisted the throttle back to zero and tried to shift into other gears. Even though the shifter was working, the bike would not engage into any gear and was moving only under it's own momentum. There was a grinding noise which was prevalent. I coasted to a stop and tried shifting gears again, but could not get the bike to engage. I heard loud grinding noises when trying to put the bike in gear and then it would stall. After being towed, the bmw dealer confirmed clutch spine failure as root cause. There are numerous incidents of this failure documented on the internet, especially on the r1100s. In looking at the failure itself, one can see that the input spline shaft teeth are about 3/4 sheared as are all the splines on the clutch mating plate except for the ends furthest away from the input shaft. This is a design error since the input shaft is 8-10mm short and does not fully engage the full length of the clutch mating splines. Therefore load and torque are not equally distributed amongst the full length of all the spines, which results in premature failure. It also appears that the input shaft was not correctly aligned to the chassis upon initial build. The fact that the design does not fully mate all the splines and that it wasn't aligned correctly when built are the root cause for this failure at less than 34,000 miles. Not only is this a costly repair since the entire bike needs to be dis-assembled, it also has the possibility of creating a fatal safety situation depending on if the fail were to occur in heavy traffic, around a curve or when carrying a passenger.
