The short answer

44 US owners have filed a safety complaint about the BMW S 1000 RR (model years 2010-2016, 2018, 2020-2025). The most reported areas are engine and cooling (41%), electrical and instruments (23%), fuel system (14%). Half of the complaints that gave a mileage happened before 3,902 miles.

The bike has had 14 safety recalls. Recall work is free at any BMW dealer, so check your VIN (link under each recall below).

Ignition Lock May Fail and Cause Engine Stall (electrical and instruments): 2020-2026 · 26V478000 · 2026-07-23 · 16,736 bikes

Problem. BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2024 S 1000 RR US, and 2020-2026 S 1000 RR motorcycles. The vibration of the motorcycle may cause an internal component of the ignition lock to fail, which can cause the instrument display, rear lighting, and other systems such as ABS to fail, or cause an engine stall while riding.

Risk. An engine stall or failure of electrical systems such as the instrument display or ABS, increases the risk of a crash.

Fix. The remedy is currently under development. Owner notification letters were mailed September 18, 2026. Additional letters will be sent once the remedy is available. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417. Vehicle Identification Numbers (VINs) involved in this recall will become searchable on NHTSA.gov beginning September 14, 2026.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

Part made by: GKS GmbH Schliess Systems, Germany

How the problem was found.

  • In April 2025, BMW became aware of an increasing number of ignition locks on S 1000 RR models replaced under warranty. An engineering investigation was initiated. Field parts were requested to be returned for analysis. At that time, the rate of occurrence in the field was less than 0.1%.
  • Between May and November, parts were returned, and analyzed. Preliminary information suggested that vibration-induced wear on the contact pin within the ignition lock could occur. However, further analysis was necessary to understand specific conditions in which vibration could play a role and, if so, the specific parameters or characteristics involved with vibration. Further investigations were …
  • Between November 2025 and April 2026, the investigation continued, and was focused on analysis of the wear process of the contact pin.
  • In April 2026, a detailed analysis involving field data, and statistical forecasting, was initiated. By June, the rate of occurrence in the field was less than approximately 0.5%, which indicated that, as mileage increased, the rate of occurrence could also increase.
  • On July 16, 2026, BMW decided to conduct a voluntary safety recall. BMW has not received any reports, nor is BMW otherwise aware, of any accidents or injuries related to this issue.

Oil Cooler Hoses May Leak (engine and cooling): 2020 · 19V697000 · 2019-10-02 · 416 bikes

Problem. BMW of North America, LLC (BMW) is recalling certain 2020 S 1000 RR motorcycles. The oil cooler hoses may not be attached properly to the oil pipes, possibly resulting in an oil leak.

Risk. Leaking oil may drip in the path of the rear tire, increasing the risk of a crash.

Fix. BMW will notify owners, and dealers will replace the oil cooler assembly, including the hoses, free of charge. Owners may contact BMW customer service at 1-800-525-7417.

From the maker's defect report to NHTSA

What the new part or software changes. Recall component – oil cooler assembly (8 356 820)

Part made by: Metallwerk Biebighäuser Gmbh, Germany

How the problem was found.

  • On July 19, 2019, BMW became aware of an incident in Japan involving a Model Year 2020 BMW S 1000 RR. The rider noticed smoke due to oil on the exhaust manifold. The rider alleged that the motorcycle did not experience adverse handling or control. Nevertheless, BMW initiated a review based upon this incident. The supplier was contacted and the part was returned for analysis.
  • On September 13, 2019, BMW became aware of an incident in Germany involving a Model Year 2020 BMW S 1000 RR, in which it was also reported that oil had contacted the exhaust manifold and created smoke.
  • On September 23, 2019, BMW and the supplier conducted a joint review of this issue. The review concluded that the oil cooler hoses may not have been attached to the oil pipes in a sufficient manner to preclude a leak.
  • On September 27, 2019, BMW decided to conduct a voluntary safety recall. BMW has not received any reports, nor is BMW otherwise aware of any accidents or injuries related to this issue.
